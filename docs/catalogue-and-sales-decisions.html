Catalogue & sales:
the decisions we need

This document lists the decisions to make before Dunia Media's media catalogue is rebuilt. Each question shows the choices, the recommended answer, and what each choice affects. It is written to be read together: the plain-language parts for the owner, the technical notes for the developer.

The recommendation at a glance

If every recommendation is accepted, this is what Dunia Media's catalogue and sales will look like.

  1. One product catalogue: every media and creator offer, plus packages. Services and training stay as website content until they are billed as a fee line.
  2. Dunia Media is the seller: one invoice, one payment flow, and media owners appear as offer information.
  3. Quote first, order later: an accepted quote becomes an order; online checkout is added later without changing the catalogue.
  4. Honest prices: real prices live in price lists; "from" prices are display only, and "on request" offers can never be sold for RM0.
  5. Rate-card PDFs stay: official documents do not disappear; the unused rate table is deleted.
  6. Capacity survives: inventory windows stay as the calendar; capacity holds become atomic and expire on their own.

A

Business basics

Who sells, how money comes in, and what we sell.

A1Business

Whose name is on the invoice?

When a customer accepts a quote, whose name is shown as the seller, and who receives the payment? This decides the invoice shape, the payment flow, and who is responsible for tax.

Impact

A. One invoice, one payment flow, one tax record. No owner payout system — settlements happen outside the system. This is the simplest option and matches what the order package can do today.

B or C. Every order line must know its seller, and the system must record payments to owners. The order package does not support this; it would have to be built.

Technical note

aiarmada/orders stores one owner per order and forces every item to that owner. There is no seller model, line split, or payout. Changing this later means rewriting the order layer.

A2Business

When does the customer pay?

Can a customer buy online right away, or must they go through a quote first?

Impact

A. Focuses on what actually happens today: the customer asks for a quote, we confirm availability, then accept the booking. Orders and invoices are still recorded. Checkout can be added without changing the catalogue.

B. Needs a payment gateway, tax, refunds, live availability, and failed-payment handling before a single booking is tested.

C. Two flows to maintain; a risk that neither matures.

Technical note

Orders can be created without a cart or checkout, with free-form lines and a calculated total. The checkout package depends on orders, not the other way around. Building checkout first means building the payment path before any product has a real price.

A3Business

What belongs in the sales catalogue?

This catalogue becomes the only list of things that can be sold and put into a quote.

Impact

A. Services and training stay as website content until they are actually billed as a fee line. Creators already use the same offer structure, so there is no second catalogue.

C. Services and training have no price, no unit, and no media profile today. They would become empty products that clutter search and admin.

Technical note

Eight services and all training have no price or SKU field. A quote today can only carry offers; extra lines are free text at zero price. Making them products now creates catalogue rows that cannot be quoted correctly.

B

Products & pricing

What one product is, how prices are shown, and how they are calculated.

B1Business

What is the real unit being sold?

Today the "from" price mixes units: RM100 might be one radio spot, RM30,000 a month for a KLIA screen, RM150,000 a year for a static billboard, and RM200,000 for one campaign package. Prices cannot be compared or calculated if the units are not the same.

Impact

A. Prices become comparable, quotable, and calculable. It requires deciding a unit for 53 offers, but only once.

B. Prices stay display-only; the system does not know what the RM100 is for.

C. Works if every sale really is one campaign, but loses the ability to compare spots and months.

Technical note

The pricing engine stores one price per unit and integer quantities. It has no concept of CPM, GRP, or fractional units. The unit must be set on the product or variant, not in rate-card text.

B2Business

What happens to the "from" price?

The "from" price is the lowest number on the rate card, not the real price. It is useful for search and display, but it must not become the transaction price.

Impact

A. Budget filters and "From RM..." messages stay. The real price lives in the price list and cannot be used by mistake.

B. Every "on request" offer becomes RM0 and can be sold for free.

C. Loses the budget filter and the early information that helps customers.

Technical note

A product price is required and defaults to 0; there is no "no price" state. A mixed-unit "from" number stored as the product price will corrupt quotes and checkout.

B3Business

How are "on request" offers handled?

Almost half of today's offers have no public price. The system must tell "free" apart from "price not set yet".

Impact

A. The catalogue stays complete, and no offer is sold for RM0.

B. A customer could "buy" an offer worth hundreds of thousands for zero.

C. 21 offers disappear from search and the directory.

Technical note

The pricing engine treats 0 as a valid price. A pricing_mode flag on the media profile must block adding to cart or checkout and point people to the quote flow.

B4Technical

Do we need variants?

A variant is a choice inside one product, for example 30s/60s duration, morning/evening daypart, or video/audio format. It is not a separate line on the rate-card PDF.

Impact

A. One product page with clear choices; each choice can have its own price.

B. The catalogue grows with small products that are hard to maintain.

C. Choices like duration and daypart cannot be represented.

Technical note

Variants have their own price, SKU, and capacity identity. They fit choices the customer actually makes, not every line of text in a rate document.

B5Business

When does a package become a composite product?

A composite product combines several offers and calculates price and availability from its components.

Impact

A. Package price, percentage discounts, and component availability can be calculated; it fits packages across media owners.

B. Simple, but the package does not know its components and cannot check availability.

C. No extra work, but the need for packages will come back.

Technical note

Composite packages support nested components, per-component pricing, percentage discounts, and availability by variant. Rewriting a package as a fixed price later means losing all of this.

B6Technical

What happens to the rate-card PDF?

The official rate card today is a PDF attached to the offer. The structured rate table only copies the "from" price and is never read by the system.

Impact

A. The official document stays for customers; no duplicate data; structured prices are added only when confirmed.

B. Two price sources that can disagree.

C. 15 rate cards must be re-entered by hand before launch.

Technical note

rate_card_lines only copies price_from, and no code reads it. The PDF is attached through the media library and can stay on the product profile.

B7Business

When do real prices go in?

A real price is a rate we are willing to quote and confirm, not the "from" number on a PDF.

Impact

A. Quotes start using real prices for offers that are settled; the rest stay negotiated.

B. Quotes stay RM0 until later.

C. The pricing engine is never used, so it is never tested.

Technical note

Prices can carry dates (start/end), so 2026 rates can go in now and old rates stay as history. Unconfirmed prices do not need to be filled in.

C

Catalogue & data

Catalogue structure, media owners, creators, and old data.

C1Technical

One product per offer?

Does each media offer (for example 988 FM, KLIA T1) become one product, or does each slot in the rate card become its own product?

Impact

A. Matches the 53 offers today and is easy to search.

B. Could reach hundreds of products and become hard to maintain.

C. Too coarse; customers cannot compare offers.

Technical note

An offer today has one placement and one inventory window. Slots are not modelled structurally; they are only text in the PDF or specifications.

C2Business

Where is the media owner stored?

A media owner (TV3, KLIA, 988 FM) can own the product, or simply be information on the offer.

Impact

A. Search across all owners works; packages across owners are possible.

B. Packages across owners become impossible, and every read needs an owner context.

C. Loses attribution and owner filtering.

Technical note

If the owner owns the product, packages cannot cross owners. Owner mode also forces an owner context on every public read, which does not suit an open catalogue.

C3Business

Creator offers

Creator offers (for example "Demo Creator 1") already use the same offer structure, with a link to the creator profile.

Impact

A. One search and one quote flow for media and creators.

B. Two catalogues and two flows.

C. Creators disappear from search and the directory.

Technical note

Creators already use media_offering with creator_profile_id and the unit "post". A separate product type would duplicate the catalogue and the quote flow.

C4Technical

What fields does the media profile need?

The media profile stores what does not belong to a generic product: owner, medium, unit, pricing status, search labels, and documents.

Impact

A. Every filter and public view has a clear field.

B. Key filters depend on JSON; harder to index and validate.

C. Extra schema work, but doable.

Technical note

Search fields like medium, location, and availability status should be real columns. Free-form specifications and terms can stay as JSON or text.

C5Technical

What terms do we use?

Code and the database use technical terms; customers see media terms. They do not have to match.

Impact

A. One sales identity, customer terms stay friendly.

B. Two names for the same thing; confusing for new developers.

C. Media terms disappear; customers may be confused.

Technical note

Code and database use Product so there are no two sales identities. Only the presentation layer keeps the term "Media Offers".

C6Technical

Public URLs

Do existing links (for example /media/988-fm-2026-radio-digital) need to keep working?

Impact

A. No broken links; SEO and shared links survive.

B. Old links die.

C. Extra work with little benefit if the site has not launched.

Technical note

Slugs can move straight to products. A URL is a public identity, not leftover code.

C7Technical

Old data

Today's data is demo data: 53 offers, 49 rate lines, and no real quotes or bookings.

Impact

A. Clean, no leftovers, and safe because there are no real transactions.

B. Extra work for data that has no value yet.

C. No demo data to test search and quotes.

Technical note

There are no quotes, bookings, invoices, or payments in the database. A data migration adds no value; reseeding is easier to test and delete.

D

Transactions

Quotes, orders, invoices, bookings, and capacity.

D1Business

Does the quote stay its own record?

A quote has an expiry date, a price-privacy level, a signed proposal link, and a request status. An order has none of these fields.

Impact

A. The negotiation, approval, and proposal-link flow stays.

B. The existing fields and flow disappear.

C. No negotiation stage before an order.

Technical note

Orders have no expiry date, price privacy, or proposal link. Using them as quotes means rebuilding all of that on top of orders.

D2Business

How does a quote become an order?

When a customer accepts a quote, the system needs to create an order that can be invoiced.

Impact

A. Finance uses one order record; price history survives even when the catalogue changes.

B. No automatic trail; easy to make mistakes.

C. Two finance systems.

Technical note

An order can be created directly from quote data, including a unique reference number to prevent duplicates. No cart is needed.

D3Technical

Our own invoices and payments

There are invoice and payment tables now, but no records are used. The order package already provides invoices, payments, refunds, and statuses.

Impact

A. One finance source; no duplicates.

B. Two ledgers that can disagree.

C. Integration work before the basic flow is tested.

Technical note

Orders support full manual payment records and PDF invoices. A gateway can be added later without changing the order model.

D4Business

Bookings

A booking stores flight dates, creative deadlines, and operational status. An order stores money and invoices. They are not the same thing.

Impact

A. Media operations stay structured; finance stays in orders.

B. Dates and operational status lose their structure.

C. Daily operations are no longer recorded.

Technical note

Bookings, flights, and creative deadlines are media domain, not finance domain. A link to the order gives one financial reference without mixing the two models.

D5Technical

Inventory capacity

The inventory package provides atomic capacity holds, but it does not understand a calendar. The existing inventory windows hold the dates and seasons.

Impact

A. The calendar stays in the app; holds become atomic and expire automatically.

B. Dates, seasons, and context are lost.

C. No atomic or auto-expiring holds.

Technical note

A capacity pool only has resource_key, scope_key, and numbers. The calendar, seasons, and windows stay in app tables; each window maps to one pool.

D6Business

When is capacity held?

A hold stops two customers from getting the same slot. It should not be taken while a customer is only looking or trying things out.

Impact

A. Visitors do not lock inventory; real bookings are protected.

B. Abandoned plans lock inventory and block sales.

C. Risk of two bookings for the same slot.

Technical note

A hold has an expiry time and can be extended, committed, or released. It can be created in one transaction across several windows.

D7Business

Availability display

Customers like to know whether something is still available. But showing the exact remaining number reveals our commercial capacity.

Impact

A. Useful information without revealing internal numbers.

B. Numbers go stale fast and reveal capacity.

C. Customers must ask about every offer.

Technical note

Status can be calculated from the remaining capacity without showing the number. The "limited" threshold is set by the app.

E

Operations & launch

Admin, tax, currency, and how we switch over.

E1Technical

Where does admin manage the catalogue?

The products package provides a generic admin, but all of its pages and relation managers are final. Media fields are not in the generic form.

Impact

A. One place to manage products and media fields.

B. Media fields missing; admin is incomplete.

C. Two places to edit the same thing.

Technical note

Package pages and relation managers are marked final. Their form and table can be reused, so an app resource can call them and add a media section.

E2Technical

Admin permissions

There are already media.manage, quotes.manage, campaigns.manage, and so on.

Impact

A. Matches existing roles.

B. Less control.

C. Risk of unlimited access.

Technical note

The new resource can use its own permission without changing existing roles. Content editors do not need catalogue access.

E3Technical

Which packages do we install now?

Every package adds tables, admin screens, and maintenance surface.

Impact

A. Enough for the catalogue, prices, capacity, and orders.

B. Too many surfaces before the basic flow is proven.

C. No pricing, capacity, or orders.

Technical note

The products and pricing packages do not strictly require each other, but they are combined here for one catalogue that can be quoted. Checkout and tax can follow without a catalogue migration.

E4Business

Tax

The existing rate cards mention "before SST 8%". The question is when tax must be calculated automatically.

Impact

A. Clear quotes without tax-engine work.

B. Extra work before the first invoice.

C. Invoices do not match the rate.

Technical note

The pricing package only stores a tax display flag; it does not calculate tax. A separate tax engine can be installed later without changing base prices.

E5Business

Currency

All prices today are in Malaysian Ringgit.

Impact

A. Simple; no conversion.

B or C. Every price list must be duplicated; no automatic conversion.

Technical note

A price supports one currency per list. Multi-currency means one list per currency, not automatic conversion.

E6Business

How do we switch over?

The app has not launched and has no real transactions. This is the chance to cut over cleanly, not build a temporary layer.

Impact

A. Clean, no leftovers, and easier to test.

B or C. Two sources of truth; risk of conflicting data and double work.

Technical note

There is no transaction data to preserve. A phased migration only adds compatibility code that must be removed later.

E7Technical

What gets deleted?

This list decides what disappears from the code and database.

Impact

A. No old catalogue leftovers; quotes and operations stay.

B. Two sources of truth.

C. Lose the negotiation and operations flow.

Technical note

Deleted: media_offerings, offering_placements, packages, package_lines, rate_cards, rate_card_lines, invoices, payments. Kept: placements, inventory_windows, quotes, bookings, campaigns.

F

Facts that lock these decisions

These are not questions. They are facts confirmed from the code and data, and the reason the recommendations above look the way they do.

MySQL is not supported

The products package uses partial unique indexes that exist only on PostgreSQL and SQLite. Other engines fail during migration.

What it means: production uses PostgreSQL; SQLite stays for development and tests.

Orders have no per-line seller

One order has one owner, and every item is forced to that owner. There is no seller model, split, or payout.

What it means: Dunia Media must be the seller of record (A1).

A price of 0 means free

There is no "no price" state in products or price lists. Zero is a valid, payable price.

What it means: "on request" offers need a flag and a checkout block (B3).

Capacity has no calendar

A capacity pool stores only two text keys and three numbers. There are no dates, seasons, or overlap checks.

What it means: inventory windows stay in the app and become the pool scope (D5).

Products cannot practically be subclassed

Actions and relationships use the base product class, and all package admin pages are marked final.

What it means: media admin is built as an app resource that reuses the package form and table (E1).

The structured rate table is unused

Rate lines only copy the "from" price. No code reads them for display, quotes, or bookings.

What it means: the old rate table is deleted; PDFs stay; real prices go into price lists (B6).

Media owners have no login

There is one admin panel and no tenancy by party. Media owners exist as party records, not users.

What it means: the catalogue stays global and the owner becomes a profile field (C2). Owner self-service can be added later without changing the catalogue.

No CPM, GRP, or fractional units

All quantities are integers and all prices are in minor units. The pricing engine has no daypart, time sheet, or media formula.

What it means: units must be discrete (spot, month, year, package); media formulas stay in the app (B1).

What changes, what stays

Changes

  • One product catalogue replaces media_offerings.
  • Real prices are separate from display prices.
  • Quotes can become orders and invoices.
  • Capacity uses atomic holds.
  • Less custom code, more packages.

Stays

  • The Dunia Media brand and name.
  • The media directory, owner pages, and search filters.
  • Rate-card PDFs as official documents.
  • The quote flow and signed proposals.
  • Campaigns and case studies as content.
  • Existing admin roles and permissions.
